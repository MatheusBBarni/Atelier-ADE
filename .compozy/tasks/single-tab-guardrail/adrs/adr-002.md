# ADR-002: Use a broader focus-workspace product approach for the PRD

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-05-31

## Context

The approved idea framed this opportunity as a narrow single-tab guardrail for focused solo users who want fewer tab mistakes and a simpler work surface. During PRD brainstorming, research confirmed that adjacent tools such as VS Code and JetBrains position focus-oriented experiences as optional simplification layers rather than as entirely separate products. Repo context also showed that Another ADE already has adjacent work around session/tab clarity, settings discoverability, and tabbed file workflows.

Clarification answers reinforced that the primary success signal is fewer tab mistakes and that the feature should mainly serve focused existing users. However, when presented with product approaches, the user chose a broader focus-workspace release over the narrower guardrail-first MVP or a softer guidance-only path. That means the PRD should describe a product experience that is still grounded in the single-tab problem, but broader in framing, discoverability, and user-perceived coherence than the initial idea.

## Decision

The PRD will use the broader focus-workspace product approach.

This means the product requirements should:

- frame the feature as a more complete focus-oriented workspace experience rather than only a narrow safety toggle
- keep fewer tab mistakes as the lead success signal
- preserve focused existing users as the primary audience
- expand the product story beyond raw enforcement so the experience feels deliberate, understandable, and coherent as a user-facing mode or preference set
- still apply YAGNI and avoid turning the PRD into a full platform redesign

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Guardrail-first preference MVP

- **Description**: Ship the smallest possible opt-in preference focused on blocking second-tab creation and reducing wrong-tab mistakes.
- **Pros**: Fastest learning loop, cleanest scope, easiest to validate against the primary success metric.
- **Cons**: Risks feeling too narrow or overly mechanical, and may under-communicate the broader focus-workspace value users can understand.
- **Why rejected**: The user chose a broader product expression instead of the narrowest MVP framing.

### Alternative 2: Soft anti-mistake guidance only

- **Description**: Keep the default multi-tab experience and add stronger friction, messaging, or guidance around accidental extra-tab actions.
- **Pros**: Lower disruption, easier to layer into the existing product story, serves a wider share of users.
- **Cons**: Weakest fit for the primary outcome of meaningfully reducing tab mistakes for people who want a true single-surface workflow.
- **Why rejected**: It does not create a strong enough contract for the target user or the chosen product direction.

## Consequences

### Positive

- Gives the PRD a more cohesive product story than a single toggle alone.
- Creates room to specify discoverability, user education, and mode clarity as part of the experience.
- Better aligns with market precedent for focus-oriented workspace features.

### Negative

- Expands scope relative to the narrowest possible MVP.
- Increases the risk of vague product requirements if the broader framing is not kept disciplined.
- May create tension with earlier preference-only thinking and require explicit phase boundaries.

### Risks

- The broader approach could drift into a large mode redesign unless the PRD keeps the MVP sharply bounded.
- Users may still evaluate the feature primarily on whether it reduces tab mistakes, so broader framing cannot come at the expense of the core behavioral value.
- If discoverability and framing expand too much in MVP, the team may spend effort on storytelling before the core user benefit is proven.

## Implementation Notes

- The PRD should describe the broader product experience in user terms only.
- The MVP section should remain disciplined and clearly separate must-have behavior from later polish.
- If additional scope decisions materially broaden or narrow the selected approach during drafting, record them in follow-up ADRs.
