# ADR-001: Projects-First Reordering Scope for V1

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-05-31

## Context

Another ADE is evaluating drag-and-drop reordering for two candidate surfaces: projects in the persistent sidebar and tabs in the active workspace. The target user is a power user who keeps many projects and tabs open and wants the interface to preserve visual priority instead of forcing repeated re-navigation.

Research and codebase review showed that the product already has durable ordering primitives for both domains: `Project.sortIndex`, `Tab.ordinal`, and `RestoreSnapshot.tabOrder`. However, the two surfaces are not equally ready. Project ordering already maps to a stable, persistent sidebar concept. Tab ordering does not: the shipped tab chrome is horizontal, the requested idea assumes a vertical movement model, and tab order is coupled to a shared session tab namespace plus restore semantics.

The council converged on a narrow but deliberate first slice. The shared concern was not whether drag-and-drop is possible, but whether V1 should establish a trustworthy persistent-priority behavior without dragging the team into a broader workspace-customization system too early.

## Decision

V1 will scope reordering to **projects only**.

The feature will establish a deliberate persistent-priority boundary for project ordering in the sidebar and treat success as a falsifiable product hypothesis: if durable user-defined project order reduces re-navigation for power users, the product earns the right to extend the concept later.

V1 will explicitly exclude tab reordering, vertical-tab UX work, cross-surface ordering abstractions, and broader workspace-priority features such as pinning, grouping, or smart auto-sorting.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Ship project and tab reordering together

- **Description**: Add drag-and-drop reordering for projects in the sidebar and tabs in the workspace within the same release.
- **Pros**:
  - Stronger apparent parity story with established developer tools.
  - More complete narrative around user-controlled ordering.
  - Could address both high-level and in-session navigation friction at once.
- **Cons**:
  - Combines two materially different surfaces under one feature label.
  - Tab ordering is currently tied to a horizontal UI, shared tab namespace, and restore behavior.
  - Higher design, persistence, and testing complexity for the first release.
- **Why rejected**: The extra scope would blur the product learning loop, create architectural pressure before a real vertical tab surface exists, and increase the risk of trust-damaging ordering bugs.

### Alternative 2: Wait and design a broader workspace-priority system first

- **Description**: Defer reordering until the team can design projects, tabs, pinning, recents, and restore behavior as one coherent priority model.
- **Pros**:
  - Maximizes conceptual consistency across surfaces.
  - Reduces the chance of later redesign caused by piecemeal behavior.
  - Could produce a more differentiated long-term model.
- **Cons**:
  - Delays relief for an existing high-frequency annoyance.
  - Increases time-to-learning on whether manual priority is valuable at all.
  - Risks over-designing a generalized system before evidence exists.
- **Why rejected**: The team can test the core hypothesis sooner and more safely by shipping a projects-only slice with deliberate persistence semantics.

## Consequences

### Positive

- Ships the smallest coherent version of user-authored visual priority.
- Fits the current product boundary where projects are already durable, visible, and persisted.
- Creates a measurable experiment around reduced re-navigation for heavy users.
- Preserves a future path to tabs or broader priority features without committing to them now.

### Negative

- Users who mainly feel the pain inside tabs may see the release as incomplete.
- The feature may be perceived as polish if usage and behavioral impact are weak.
- The product story remains asymmetric until the tab layer gets its own durable priority surface.

### Risks

- **Trust risk**: if ordering is lost, inconsistent, or too easy to change accidentally, users will stop relying on it. **Mitigation**: prioritize durable persistence, obvious drag targets, and restore correctness.
- **Scope creep risk**: pinning, grouping, favorites, or generalized ordering logic may try to attach themselves to this release. **Mitigation**: keep V1 explicitly project-only.
- **False-strategy risk**: the feature may feel valuable without moving behavior. **Mitigation**: instrument success criteria around re-navigation reduction and repeat use of custom order.

## Implementation Notes

- Treat project ordering as an owned persistent concern, not a view-only behavior.
- Keep any move helper narrow and private to project reordering in V1.
- Avoid introducing a cross-entity reordering framework until there is validated extension pressure.
- If V2 explores tabs, require a real tab-priority surface first rather than forcing tab behavior into the current horizontal chrome.
