# ADR-002: Trust-First Repo-Oriented Landing Page PRD Scope

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-05-31

## Context

The landing page idea has already established a minimal, docs-first trailhead as the right general direction for V1. During PRD brainstorming, the user clarified the more specific product intent for the first release:

- optimize first for helping visitors understand the product
- make trust the clearest message on the page
- use a real product screenshot as the primary proof asset
- keep the first release very small
- encourage qualified visitors to open the repository after they understand and trust the product
- judge success primarily by repo visits rather than by raw install-start volume

This decision determines how the PRD should frame the page's scope, priorities, and next-step behavior.

## Decision

Define V1 as a trust-first, repo-oriented landing page.

The PRD should describe a small public surface that:

- explains what Atelier is and why it is credible
- uses a real screenshot to ground the product in visible reality
- highlights concrete, current capabilities instead of aspirational claims
- keeps the page intentionally small and easy to understand
- sends the highest-intent visitors into the repository as the primary downstream action
- supports docs and quickstart as secondary evaluation paths rather than the main product ask

The PRD should treat trust, legibility, and honest product framing as the primary business outcomes for MVP.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Workflow Explainer Page

- **Description**: Expand the page to spend more space teaching the product workflow before directing visitors deeper.
- **Pros**: Better education for unfamiliar visitors; stronger explanation of sessions, tabs, and workflow model; could reduce confusion for colder traffic.
- **Cons**: Less minimal; increases copy and design scope; risks turning the MVP into a broader storytelling page.
- **Why rejected**: The user prioritized trust and product understanding through clear proof over a larger explanatory surface.

### Alternative 2: Evaluation-to-Quickstart Bridge

- **Description**: Use the landing page to move visitors more aggressively into docs or quickstart after a brief explanation.
- **Pros**: Better activation potential; aligns with common market CTA patterns; may create more immediate trial behavior.
- **Cons**: Feels more conversion-led; asks for action sooner than the current trust and onboarding posture can fully support.
- **Why rejected**: The user chose a repo-oriented path because credibility and understanding should come before stronger activation goals.

## Consequences

### Positive

- Keeps the PRD tightly aligned with the user's explicit priorities.
- Preserves a small V1 scope that is easier to ship and easier to keep honest.
- Creates a public surface that strengthens product credibility before pushing heavier conversion behavior.
- Uses repo visits as a clearer signal of qualified developer interest than top-of-funnel clicks alone.

### Negative

- May produce fewer quickstart or install starts than a more activation-oriented page.
- Leaves some workflow explanation depth for later phases.
- Requires the repository itself to carry more of the detailed evaluation burden.

### Risks

- **Repo-first behavior may be too technical for some visitors** — Mitigate by keeping docs and quickstart visible as secondary paths.
- **Trust-first messaging may become vague if not grounded in product reality** — Mitigate by using screenshot-led proof and concrete current capabilities.
- **Success may be misread if repo visits rise but deeper evaluation does not** — Mitigate by tracking repo visits alongside docs visits and quickstart starts.

## Implementation Notes

- The PRD should remain business-focused and describe user outcomes, not technical implementation.
- MVP scope should stay narrow: trust, proof, capability clarity, and repo-oriented next step.
- Broader workflow explanation and stronger activation can be framed as later phases rather than MVP requirements.
