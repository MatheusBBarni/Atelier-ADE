# ADR-002: Use a Settings-First Appearance Baseline with System as the Lead Choice

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-05-31

## Context

The PRD for Light and Dark Theme Expansion needs a clear product approach for V1. Research shows that modern productivity and developer tools commonly present appearance choices as Light, Dark, and System. Codebase findings show that Another ADE already has a working Settings-based appearance surface, existing persistence, and a theme catalog, but the current experience feels sparse and incomplete, especially for first impressions.

During brainstorming, the user emphasized that this release should optimize first impression and perceived polish. The user also indicated that MVP needs both stronger curated presets and System behavior, while keeping the discovery path centered in Settings.

## Decision

V1 will follow a Settings-first appearance approach that combines the discipline of a preset-focused release with the completeness of System support.

The product will:

- keep appearance discovery and change management inside the existing Settings experience
- present **System** as the first choice in the theme list, followed by explicit light and dark options
- improve the overall confidence and polish of theme selection
- add a small curated set of stronger light and dark presets without expanding into broad customization

This approach intentionally avoids turning V1 into a large personalization initiative while still meeting the baseline expectation that the app can respect the user's device appearance.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Pure Baseline Completeness

- **Description**: Ship System, Light, and Dark together with curated presets as the main framing for the release.
- **Pros**: Closest to current market expectations; strongest immediate feeling of completeness.
- **Cons**: Slightly broader product framing for a quick-win release; risks encouraging extra scope around appearance parity and discoverability.
- **Why rejected**: The user preferred a more disciplined, preset-first framing rather than a broader “full baseline” positioning.

### Alternative 2: Preset-First Polish without System

- **Description**: Improve theme selection UX and add curated presets, but defer System support.
- **Pros**: Smallest and safest V1; easiest to keep bounded.
- **Cons**: More likely to feel incomplete against user expectations and competitor norms.
- **Why rejected**: The user explicitly wants System included in MVP, and research suggests its absence would weaken first impression.

### Alternative 3: Full Appearance Refresh

- **Description**: Expand V1 to include System, curated presets, additional discovery affordances, and broader appearance polish positioning.
- **Pros**: Strongest perception of polish and ambition.
- **Cons**: Highest scope risk; easier to drift beyond a disciplined MVP.
- **Why rejected**: Too broad for a feature intended to remain a focused quality release.

## Consequences

### Positive

- Aligns the product with common user expectations without over-expanding the release.
- Improves first impression for evaluators while still serving active users who want better appearance choice.
- Keeps the narrative clear: Another ADE offers a polished, intentional appearance experience rather than an open-ended theming system.

### Negative

- Adds more MVP scope than a strictly preset-only release.
- Raises user expectations that all major surfaces will feel equally complete under System, Light, and Dark choices.

### Risks

- **Scope creep risk**: users may interpret System support as justification for broader appearance work. Mitigation: keep discovery Settings-first and limit V1 to a short curated preset set.
- **Perception risk**: if System is present but the experience still feels visually uneven, the release may still look unfinished. Mitigation: define completeness around perceived polish, not only option count.
- **Priority risk**: the feature could consume more attention than its differentiation warrants. Mitigation: position it as a bounded quality milestone, not a flagship product bet.

## Implementation Notes

- Keep the product framing centered on user-facing appearance behavior and clarity of choice.
- Treat System as the lead option in the user-visible list to reinforce the preferred default behavior.
- Preserve a short, curated preset set rather than a large theme catalog.
