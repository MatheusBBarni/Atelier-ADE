# ADR-001: Scope V1 as Curated Appearance Presets and Polish

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-05-31

## Context

The app already has a functioning theme system: Settings exposes an Appearance section, theme selection is persisted, and the active theme updates the shell, editor, and terminal surfaces. The original idea was to add more themes to the app, especially light and dark options.

Research shows that appearance controls are now table stakes in modern productivity apps. Common expectations are a small, easy-to-find appearance control and a complete-feeling theme experience. The user clarified that the main goal is personal preference for all users, with a minimal quick-win V1 discovered in Settings and evaluated by usage rate.

The main product question is not whether to build a theming platform. It is how much to change in V1 without turning a polish feature into a broad customization project.

## Decision

V1 will focus on polishing the existing Appearance experience and adding a small curated set of additional appearance presets within the current theme catalog.

The scope includes:

- keeping theme selection in the existing Settings/Preferences flow
- improving the clarity and completeness of the current theme experience
- adding a limited number of curated presets, with emphasis on better light-theme coverage
- validating consistency across shell, editor, and terminal surfaces

The scope excludes for V1:

- System/Auto mode unless it proves trivially cheap during implementation planning
- custom theme creation or user-authored presets
- per-component overrides
- broader visual redesign outside appearance/theme polish

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Discoverability and default-quality only

- **Description**: Improve the existing theme picker and defaults without adding any new presets.
- **Pros**: Lowest QA cost, smallest scope, fastest to ship.
- **Cons**: May feel underwhelming if users still perceive the catalog as too thin, especially on the light-theme side.
- **Why rejected**: The user explicitly wants more theme choice, and the existing catalog-based architecture makes a small curated expansion relatively cheap.

### Alternative 2: Baseline parity plus broader appearance expansion

- **Description**: Ship System/Auto mode, more presets, richer previews, and broader appearance controls in one pass.
- **Pros**: Closer to market parity, stronger perception of completeness, more room for differentiation.
- **Cons**: More state handling, more QA surface, more risk of scope creep, and higher chance of inconsistent cross-surface behavior.
- **Why rejected**: Too large for a quick-win V1 and likely to delay delivery of the highest-confidence improvement.

## Consequences

### Positive

- Leverages existing theme infrastructure instead of introducing a new theming model.
- Improves perceived product polish for all users with limited engineering risk.
- Keeps the feature measurable through settings engagement and preset adoption.
- Creates a clear V1 boundary that can feed a later PRD and TechSpec.

### Negative

- Deferring System/Auto may leave the feature short of full market parity at launch.
- A curated preset expansion still increases QA effort across multiple surfaces.
- The feature remains mostly a parity/polish improvement rather than a differentiator.

### Risks

- **Light-mode inconsistency risk**: additional presets may expose surfaces that are not fully polished in light mode. Mitigation: require cross-surface QA for shell, editor, overlays, and terminal.
- **Scope creep risk**: requests for previews, auto mode, or custom themes may expand the work. Mitigation: keep explicit V1 exclusions in the PRD.
- **Weak impact risk**: users may care more about defaults and discoverability than extra presets. Mitigation: measure settings engagement and theme adoption, and keep the preset list short.

## Implementation Notes

- Use the existing theme catalog and theme ID persistence model rather than introducing a new preference structure.
- Treat curated appearance presets as the framing for V1, not “open-ended theming.”
- Preserve safe fallback behavior for invalid or removed theme IDs.
