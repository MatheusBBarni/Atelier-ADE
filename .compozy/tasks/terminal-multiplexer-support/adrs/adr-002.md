# ADR-002: Use a restore-first product approach for Focus Workspace continuity

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-06-01

## Context

The PRD needed a concrete product approach for Focus Workspace Continuity after clarification and research. The user prioritized fast re-entry as the primary outcome, selected solo power users as the primary audience, chose smarter restore landing as the day-one value, preferred settings-first discoverability, and said the MVP must not disrupt legacy multi-tab workflows. The strongest proof of success is higher opt-in adoption.

Research reinforced that Another ADE already has strong user-facing continuity primitives through projects, sessions, tabs, restore, commands, and Focus Workspace. It also showed that competitors already teach users to expect reliable resume and clean thread boundaries, but that deeper orchestration surfaces quickly expand scope. Given the current product boundary, the PRD needed an approach that feels meaningfully better for target users without implying deeper multiplexer control than the product can honestly deliver.

## Decision

The PRD will use a restore-first product approach for Focus Workspace continuity.

This means the MVP will emphasize:

- smarter landing into the right project, session, and tab after relaunch or return
- a clearer Focus Workspace experience for solo power users who already organize terminal complexity outside the app
- settings-first adoption and in-product trust-building rather than public repositioning first
- preservation of current flexible multi-tab workflows for users who do not opt in

The PRD should treat command shortcuts, continuity cues, and product copy as supporting elements around the restore-first core experience rather than as the primary value proposition.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Command-first return workflow

- **Description**: Center the MVP on an explicit "take me back" action during active work, prioritizing in-session switching moments over restore and relaunch moments.
- **Pros**: Strong power-user appeal, clear deliberate action, good fit for keyboard-first workflows.
- **Cons**: Weaker alignment with the chosen day-one value, less impact on relaunch behavior, and a less direct answer to the restore problem the user prioritized.
- **Why rejected**: Not the best match for the clarified user value hierarchy.

### Alternative 2: Focus-mode clarity first

- **Description**: Lead with better settings copy, state cues, and mode framing before materially improving where users land.
- **Pros**: Lowest delivery risk, clearest story, minimal disruption.
- **Cons**: Too shallow to move adoption meaningfully if the actual pain is slow or noisy return to context.
- **Why rejected**: Unlikely to create enough practical value for the target power-user cohort.

## Consequences

### Positive

- Aligns the MVP closely to the chosen primary outcome of fast re-entry.
- Creates a more concrete benefit for solo power users than messaging-only changes.
- Preserves room for later command-driven or awareness-driven improvements if adoption proves strong.

### Negative

- May underdeliver for users who feel the strongest pain during active in-session switching rather than relaunch.
- Requires careful product wording so smarter restore does not sound like live external state recovery.
- Leaves some immediate power-user flow improvements for later phases.

### Risks

- Adoption may stay low if settings-first discovery is too passive.
- Users may not perceive the improvement unless the new landing behavior is obvious and trustworthy.
- The team could optimize restore behavior while underestimating demand for active return workflows.

## Implementation Notes

- Keep the PRD centered on user-facing restore and re-entry outcomes rather than on deeper workflow ambition.
- Preserve current flexible workflows for non-opted-in users.
- Use future phases to explore command-first or awareness-first expansions only if opt-in adoption validates the restore-first core.
